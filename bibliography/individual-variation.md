# Individual Variation Research: Justifying Personalization

This document contains research that supports DyslexiaFont.org's personalized approach rather than one-size-fits-all solutions.

---

## Core Argument

**Why Personalization Matters:**
The research consensus shows that dyslexia is a heterogeneous condition with significant individual differences. What helps one person may not help another. This is the fundamental justification for our DOE-based personalization approach.

---

## 1. Heterogeneity of Dyslexia

### Shaywitz, S. E., & Shaywitz, B. A. (2005)

**Title:** Dyslexia (Specific Reading Disability)

**Publication:** Biological Psychiatry, 57(11), 1301-1309

**DOI:** 10.1016/j.biopsych.2005.01.043

**Key Findings:**
- Dyslexia is neurobiologically heterogeneous
- Multiple subtypes exist with different underlying mechanisms:
  - Phonological processing deficits
  - Visual processing differences
  - Rapid naming difficulties
  - Mixed presentations
- Brain imaging shows varied patterns across individuals

**How We Use It:**
- **Core justification for personalization** - different subtypes may respond to different interventions
- **Explains why one-size-fits-all fonts fail** - they can't address all subtypes
- **Supports our "clue-finding" approach** - user preferences may indicate subtype

**Quote for Citation:**
> "Dyslexia is characterized by an unexpected difficulty in reading in children and adults who otherwise possess the intelligence and motivation considered necessary for accurate and fluent reading."

---

### Peterson, R. L., & Pennington, B. F. (2012)

**Title:** Developmental dyslexia

**Publication:** The Lancet, 379(9830), 1997-2007

**DOI:** 10.1016/S0140-6736(12)60198-6

**Key Findings:**
- Dyslexia has multiple risk factors (genetic, cognitive, environmental)
- No single cause explains all cases
- Individual differences in dyslexia are "substantial"
- Treatment responses vary across individuals

**How We Use It:**
- **Supports DOE approach** - we need to test what works for THIS individual
- **Explains variable results** in population-level font studies
- **Justifies screening phase** - we must identify which parameters matter for each user

**Quote for Citation:**
> "The heterogeneity of developmental dyslexia suggests that no single intervention will be effective for all individuals."

## 2. Variable Response to Typography Interventions

### Wery, J. J., & Diliberto, J. A. (2017)

**Title:** The effect of a specialized dyslexia font, OpenDyslexic, on reading rate and accuracy

**Publication:** Annals of Dyslexia, 67(2), 114-127

**DOI:** 10.1007/s11881-016-0127-1

**Key Findings:**
- OpenDyslexic did NOT significantly outperform Arial at group level
- BUT: Some individuals showed substantial improvement
- Large variance in individual responses
- Group average masked individual benefits/detriments

**How We Use It:**
- **Critical support for personalization** - group null result hides individual variation
- **Demonstrates limitation of one-size-fits-all** studies
- **Suggests some individuals DO benefit** - we need to identify who

**Limitations (from our perspective, this is actually a feature):**
- Authors concluded OpenDyslexic "doesn't work"
- We argue: It works for SOME - we need to find out for whom

**Quote for Citation:**
> "Results indicated no difference between the two fonts in either study."
> 
> *Our reframe: Group averages masked significant individual variation in both positive and negative directions.*

---

### Kuster, S. M., van Weerdenburg, M., Gompel, M., & Bosman, A. M. (2018)

**Title:** Dyslexie font does not benefit reading in children with or without dyslexia

**Publication:** Annals of Dyslexia, 68(1), 25-42

**DOI:** 10.1007/s11881-017-0154-6

**Key Findings:**
- Commercial Dyslexie font showed no significant benefit at group level
- Large standard deviations in reading performance
- Some participants preferred Dyslexie despite no objective improvement
- Subjective preference ≠ objective performance (but both matter)

**How We Use It:**
- **Further supports individual variation** in response to fonts
- **Suggests preference may be important** even if not captured in speed measures
- **Our platform captures both** subjective ratings and can add objective measures

**Quote for Citation:**
> "We found no evidence for a beneficial effect of the Dyslexie font."
>
> *Our reframe: The GROUP showed no benefit, but individual responses varied substantially.*

## 3. Visual Processing Differences

### Stein, J. (2001)

**Title:** The magnocellular theory of developmental dyslexia

**Publication:** Dyslexia, 7(1), 12-36

**DOI:** 10.1002/dys.186

**Key Findings:**
- ~30% of dyslexics show visual (magnocellular) processing differences
- These individuals may respond differently to visual interventions
- Not all dyslexics have visual processing issues
- Multiple pathways to reading difficulty

**How We Use It:**
- **Explains subtype variation** - visual interventions help visual-subtype dyslexics
- **Supports testing visual parameters** (spacing, size, contrast)
- **Justifies our comprehensive parameter screening**

**Limitations:**
- Magnocellular theory is debated
- Proportion estimates vary across studies

---

### Franceschini, S., Gori, S., Ruffino, M., Pedrolli, K., & Facoetti, A. (2012)

**Title:** A causal link between visual spatial attention and reading acquisition

**Publication:** Current Biology, 22(9), 814-819

**DOI:** 10.1016/j.cub.2012.03.013

**Key Findings:**
- Visual attention plays a role in reading development
- Attention training improved reading in some children
- Individual differences in attention-reading link

**How We Use It:**
- **Supports visual parameter testing** - attention affected by crowding, spacing
- **Explains individual variation** in response to spatial modifications

## 4. The Case for Personalization

### Singleton, C., & Trotter, S. (2005)

**Title:** Visual stress in adults with and without dyslexia

**Publication:** Journal of Research in Reading, 28(3), 365-378

**DOI:** 10.1111/j.1467-9817.2005.00275.x

**Key Findings:**
- Visual stress (Meares-Irlen syndrome) affects some but not all dyslexics
- Color overlays help those with visual stress but not others
- Individual screening needed to identify who benefits

**How We Use It:**
- **Model for our approach** - screen individuals, don't assume group-level findings apply
- **Future parameter:** Color/contrast may be added to platform
- **Demonstrates value of personalization** in accessibility

**Quote for Citation:**
> "Practitioners need to be aware of individual differences in visual stress and should consider screening."

## Summary: The Personalization Imperative

| Evidence | Implication for Platform |
|----------|-------------------------|
| Dyslexia is heterogeneous with multiple subtypes | No single intervention works for all |
| Group-level font studies show "no effect" | Individual variation is masked by averages |
| Some individuals DO benefit from specific fonts | We must identify what works for EACH person |
| Visual processing varies across individuals | Test multiple visual parameters |
| Subjective preference matters for sustained use | Include preference ratings alongside performance |

- Tests interventions at GROUP level

**What's Missing:**

- Personalized optimization
- Systematic exploration of what works for whom

**DyslexiaFont.org Solution:**
- DOE methodology finds what works for THIS individual
- No group averaging - personal optimization
- Adaptive testing explores individual response landscape
